DESCRIPTION

i.coregistration.opencl finds control points between an imagery group and a reference imagery group, and writes them as the group's control points for i.rectify (or v.rectify). The images are matched on an OpenCL device (GPU or CPU) by a learnt matcher, with weights read directly from their published files; the matches are refined to a fraction of a cell by normalized cross-correlation, and a robust model fitted per tile makes the remaining outliers (objects that moved between the images, mismatches) inactive control points. Neither Python nor PyTorch is needed at run time.

The matching method is chosen with matcher: Efficient LoFTR (eloftr, the default), a detector-free matcher that finds matches over the whole image, texture-poor areas included, or DISK keypoints matched by nearest neighbour and filtered with AdaLAM (disk), which keeps the matches that agree with a local affine transformation around confident seed matches. Both give the same outputs and go through the same filters, refinement and robust estimation. On the evaluation pairs (aerial basemaps of 0.5 m), Efficient LoFTR with the refinement gave 13 to 18 times as many control points as DISK, over every part of the images, and smaller leave-one-out residuals; DISK did better between images of very different radiometry and left fewer matches on moved objects (see tests/eval/README.md).

The module is a translation to C and OpenCL of the RS_coreg_LoFTR workflow (DISK features, AdaLAM matching, control points, thin plate spline warp with gdalwarp), made for images that are already roughly georeferenced in the same project: aerial and satellite basemaps of different dates, a hyperspectral scene and a very high resolution reference, and so on.

The group (the image to co-register) and the reference (or their subgroup and reference_subgroup) must each contain exactly three raster maps, used as red, green and blue in group order (see i.group). Both are read over the current computational region, on its grid: set the region to the reference (for instance g.region raster=...) and make sure that the image to co-register covers it. Bands within 0..255 are used as they are (as the repository reads its 8-bit images); others, or all bands with percentile, are stretched linearly to 0..255. Cells that are NULL in any band yield no keypoint.

Tiles

The matcher runs on tiles of tile_size cells (1024 by default), with an overlap of tile_overlap cells, taken at the same place in both images: since the images are already roughly aligned, each tile of the image is matched against the same tile of the reference only. This keeps the matches local, lets images of any size be processed in the memory of one tile, and spreads the control points over the whole region. With disk, each tile yields up to features keypoints per image (2048, as the repository).

DISK

DISK (Tyszkiewicz et al., 2020) is a U-Net of 5x5 convolutions with instance normalization and PReLU gates, whose output holds a 128-value descriptor and a detection score for every cell. Keypoints are the cells that are strict maxima of the score over 5x5 windows and positive, the features highest of them; their descriptors are normalized to unit length. This is the inference procedure of kornia.feature.DISK, and on the same tile the keypoints, scores and descriptors agree with it to single precision (see tests/reference_compare.py).

The checkpoint is one of the two published DISK models (4.2 MiB each), chosen with model:

  • depth (depth-save.pth, default, as the repository): trained with depth supervision, more precise;
  • epipolar (epipolar-save.pth): trained with epipolar supervision, with keypoints on everything that can be matched, thin structures and object edges included.

Without checkpoint, the model is looked for in weights_dir (by default $DISK_MODELS_DIR, or ~/models/disk) and downloaded there when missing, unless the -o flag is given. Both the current PyTorch zip format and the legacy format these files use are read.

Matching and AdaLAM

Each keypoint of the image is matched to the nearest descriptor of the reference, scored by the ratio of the squared distances to the nearest and second nearest. AdaLAM (Cavalli et al., 2020) then picks seed matches, the best-scored ones (ratio below 0.8 squared) within circles whose area is the tile's keypoint extent divided by area_ratio, gathers around each seed the matches lying within search_expansion times that radius in both images, and fits a local affine transformation to each neighbourhood with a confidence-based RANSAC of ransac_iters hypotheses followed by a least-squares refit. Matches of neighbourhoods with a confidence of at least min_confidence and at least min_inliers inliers are kept. With -m, seeds are taken among mutual nearest neighbours only. The defaults are those of the repository (kornia's with a search expansion of 16, 256 hypotheses and no mutual nearest neighbour constraint), and the procedure follows kornia.feature.match_adalam step by step: on the same keypoints, both return the same matches. DISK keypoints have no orientation or scale, so AdaLAM's orientation and scale agreement tests do not apply.

Efficient LoFTR

Efficient LoFTR (Wang et al., 2024) is a detector-free, semi-dense matcher in the line of LoFTR (Sun et al., 2021). Both tiles, converted to grey (0.2989 R + 0.5870 G + 0.1140 B), go through a RepVGG backbone (Ding et al., 2021) whose 3x3, 1x1 and identity branches are folded into single 3x3 convolutions when the weights are read. Its features at one eighth of the resolution are transformed by four layers of self- and cross-attention between the two images, computed on tokens aggregated over 4x4 cells (with a 2D rotary position encoding in the self-attention). Every cell of 8x8 pixels of the image is then matched to the cell of the reference with the highest dual-softmax confidence, when that confidence exceeds 0.2 and the choice is mutual; the score matrix between all cells of the two tiles is processed by blocks of rows, never stored whole. Each such match is refined at full resolution, with features fused from the coarse, quarter and half resolution levels: first to the best pair of pixels between an 8x8 window of the image and a 10x10 window of the reference, then to a fraction of a cell in the reference by the expectation of a 3x3 correlation. The dist of a match is one minus its coarse confidence.

The weights are those published by the authors, converted by Hugging Face (zju-community/efficientloftr, Apache License 2.0, 61 MiB). Without checkpoint, model.safetensors is looked for in weights_dir (by default $ELOFTR_MODELS_DIR, or ~/models/eloftr) and downloaded there when missing, unless -o is given. Tile sides are rounded up to multiples of 32 cells. AdaLAM and the options of the DISK and AdaLAM sections do not apply. On the same tile, the coarse matches are those of the authors' code, and the fine positions agree with it within 1e-4 cell (99 % within 1e-6), but for float ties in the first fine stage (see tests/reference_compare_eloftr.py).

Filters and refinement

Images of different dates show objects that moved: a vehicle, an aircraft or a ship gives matches that agree with each other but not with the ground. max_shift drops matches whose shift exceeds that distance (in map units), and tolerance those whose shift differs from the median shift of their tile by more than that along either axis.

DISK keypoints lie on cell centres, so its matches are only as precise as one cell. Efficient LoFTR refines its matches to a fraction of a cell, but on aerial basemaps its estimate leans towards whole cells (median error 0.3-0.4 cell on synthetic sub-cell shifts, against 0.1-0.2 cell after the refinement below). So, unless -r is given, each match is refined in the reference to a fraction of a cell: the 15x15-cell grey window of the image around the keypoint is correlated (normalized cross-correlation) with reference windows shifted by up to two cells, and the peak is fitted by a parabola along each axis. Matches whose peak lies on the edge of the search area, or whose windows are flat or meet NULL cells, keep their whole-cell position. -s, which asked for this refinement before it became the default, is deprecated.

Matches sharing a reference cell (found in two overlapping tiles, or by two keypoints) are merged into one control point, at the mean of their positions, as the repository does.

Robust estimation

AdaLAM checks each match against its neighbourhood only. Objects that moved between the two images, when they are large enough (an aircraft at half a metre), give clusters of matches that agree with each other and pass. So a model is fitted to the matches of each tile (tile_model: an affinity by default, or a similarity or a homography), after the filters and the refinement, and the matches that do not fit it become inactive control points: they stay in the POINTS file with status 0, which i.rectify and v.rectify skip, and in the points map with inlier 0. Inliers and outliers are merged apart. estimator chooses the method, superansac by default; none keeps every match active (the behaviour of the RS_coreg_LoFTR workflow).

  • ransac draws random minimal samples (Fischler and Bolles, 1981), keeps the model with the most inliers and refits it to them by least squares.
  • magsac scores each model by the MAGSAC++ quality (Barath et al., 2020), which marginalizes the noise scale up to a maximum instead of counting points under a hard threshold, and refits the best model by least squares weighted by the MAGSAC++ weights, iterated; each point gets a weight between 0 and 1.
  • gcransac labels inliers and outliers by a minimum graph cut with a spatial coherence term between neighbouring matches (Graph-Cut RANSAC, Barath and Matas, 2018; graph construction after Kolmogorov and Zabih, 2004, maximum flow by Dinic, 1970), so that a coherent cluster is kept or rejected together.
  • superansac combines them: samples drawn first among the best-ranked matches (PROSAC, Chum and Matas, 2005, by the matcher's confidence), MAGSAC++ quality, graph-cut local optimization, final weighted least squares.

Every estimator rejects samples that do not determine a model and models that reverse the orientation or scale the image by more than a factor of two, and counts the matches lying within 8 cells of each other once (the independent inliers of Ivashechkin et al., 2021), so that a dense cluster cannot outvote the ground. threshold is the largest distance of an inlier to the model, in map units (3 cells by default); the MAGSAC++ noise scale goes up to a third of it. A tile where no model can be fitted has all its matches made inactive.

The threshold trades the outliers left against the good points lost. On basemaps of half a metre, the default removed the matches of moved aircraft and halved the 90th percentile of the leave-one-out residuals; two cells removed more outliers but also points of genuine local distortion that one model per tile cannot follow. Between a hyperspectral scene of 11.3 m and basemaps averaged to its grid, the cross-modal matches have a heavier tail: one to one and a half cells brought the 90th percentile of the leave-one-out residuals from about two cells to about a third of a cell, where three cells left it unchanged.

With region_model, a second model is fitted to the active control points over the whole region (a similarity, an affinity, a homography or a polynomial of order 2 or 3), with the same estimator, and its outliers become inactive too. The module then reports the root mean square of the inlier residuals and, for the linear models, of their leave-one-out residuals (each point against the least-squares model fitted without it). Keep it for regions whose images differ by a smooth distortion: over a mosaic with seams, a global polynomial rejects the points of whole patches that are simply off by a little.

Output

The control points are written to the group's POINTS file, source east/north (the cell's position in the group's current georeferencing) and reference east/north, active unless rejected by the robust estimation, and the current project and mapset become the group's target: i.rectify with -t (thin plate spline, the repository's gdalwarp -tps) or a polynomial order then warps the group onto the reference, and v.rectify moves vector maps drawn on the image. With -n, the group is left as it is.

The points vector map holds the control points at their reference positions, with the attribute columns:

  • src_east, src_north: position in the image;
  • ref_east, ref_north: position in the reference;
  • dx, dy, shift: shift from the image to the reference, in map units;
  • dist: AdaLAM inverse confidence of the neighbourhood with disk, one minus the coarse matching confidence with eloftr (lower is better);
  • tile: tile of the match;
  • refined: 1 when refined to a fraction of a cell by the cross-correlation (0 with -r);
  • matches: matches merged into the point;
  • weight: weight of the robust estimation, 0..1 (MAGSAC++ weight relative to a zero residual; 1 with estimator=none);
  • residual: distance to the last fitted model, in map units (NULL with estimator=none);
  • inlier: 1 for the active control points, 0 for the rejected ones.

The module reports the number of matches, those dropped by each filter and by the robust estimation, and the median shift of the active control points with its median absolute deviation.

NOTES

OpenCL device

device selects the first GPU (gpu), the first CPU device (cpu), or the first GPU and else the first CPU (auto); platform restricts the search to platforms whose name contains the given text. A GPU is strongly recommended. With Mesa's rusticl, set RUSTICL_ENABLE to the driver, e.g. RUSTICL_ENABLE=radeonsi.

Performance

DISK takes about 300 billion multiply-adds per million cells and per image. On one 1024 x 1024 tile, a laptop CPU (PoCL, 4 cores) took about four minutes for both images. The memory needed on the device grows with the tile area: about 1.2 GiB for 1024-cell tiles.

On an AMD Radeon Pro WX 7100 GPU (Mesa rusticl), a 1024 x 1024 tile took about four seconds for both images, matching included (25 tiles in 1 min 45 s). The first run after an installation is a few minutes longer, while the driver compiles the kernels; it keeps them in its cache afterwards. With --verbose, the time spent on the device and in matching is reported at the end.

Efficient LoFTR took about two seconds per 1024 x 1024 tile on the same GPU, and its matching on the host is almost free (25 tiles in about 1 min 25 s with the refinement, 1 min 5 s with -r), for 15 to 20 times as many control points as DISK, spread over the whole tile. It needs about 2 GiB of device memory for 1024-cell tiles.

Debugging aids

With the environment variable I_COREGISTRATION_OPENCL_DUMP set to a folder, each tile's DISK inputs, keypoints with their scores and descriptors, and AdaLAM matches are written there; tests/reference_compare.py compares them with kornia in a Python environment with PyTorch. With eloftr, the grey inputs, the backbone, transformer and fine features and the matches are written, and tests/reference_compare_eloftr.py compares them stage by stage with transformers. With I_COREGISTRATION_OPENCL_PROFILE set, every OpenCL kernel is timed and the totals per kernel are reported at the end (this slows the run down).

EXAMPLES

Two basemaps of different dates

Co-register a Google basemap to an ESRI one, dropping matches on aircraft that moved between the two dates (more than 5 m, or 2 m away from their tile's median, then those that do not fit an affinity per tile), then warp it (the thin plate spline uses the active control points only):

i.group group=esri input=esri.1,esri.2,esri.3
i.group group=google input=google.1,google.2,google.3
g.region raster=esri.1 -p
i.coregistration.opencl group=google reference=esri points=gcp_google \
    max_shift=5 tolerance=2
i.rectify -t group=google extension=_coreg method=linear -c

DISK keypoints

The same pair with DISK and AdaLAM, which give fewer control points (none on tarmac, bare soil and other texture-poor areas) but fare better between images of very different radiometry:

i.coregistration.opencl group=google reference=esri points=gcp_google \
    matcher=disk max_shift=5 tolerance=2

Hyperspectral scene to a very high resolution reference

Match a hyperspectral scene (three bands near red, green and blue) against a WorldView-3 pansharpened RGB image, on the reference grid, with a robust estimation tighter than the default (cross-modal matches have a heavier tail: here 5 m, about one and a half cells of the 3.3 m scene), and look at the active control points before warping:

i.group group=hymap_rgb input=hymap.28,hymap.17,hymap.8
i.group group=wv3_rgb input=wv3.5,wv3.3,wv3.2
g.region raster=wv3.5 -p
i.coregistration.opencl group=hymap_rgb reference=wv3_rgb \
    points=gcp_hymap -n threshold=5
v.db.univar map=gcp_hymap column=shift where="inlier = 1"
